Does the simulation beat “just use form”?

Verdict

Likely starters are players the model gave at least a 50% chance to start before the gameweek: the pool you actually pick from. Reserves who score 0 are easy to predict and would flatter every number.

Against the benchmarks

Each prediction is compared on the same player-gameweeks as two simple rules: points per appearance so far, and the average of the last three appearances. Error is the average gap between predicted and actual points (lower is better). Ranking is how well the order of players within a gameweek matches the order of their actual scores (1 = identical, 0 = random; higher is better).

Picks and captaincy

Each gameweek, from the same pool of likely starters with history: the actual points of the model’s top 10 against points-per-game’s top 10, and of each one’s captain choice (doubled).

Are the probabilities honest?

Predictions are grouped by how likely the model said an event was; each dot is one group, placed at the average prediction (across) and how often it actually happened (up). Dots on the diagonal mean the probabilities can be taken at face value. Dot size shows how many predictions are in the group. 2025/26 season.

Is the spread of outcomes right?

For every player-gameweek, where the actual score fell inside the predicted range, in tenths. If the ranges are honest, each tenth holds 10% of outcomes (the line). Too much at the edges would mean overconfidence; too much in the middle, vagueness.

How this was tested

  • 2025/26 (37 gameweeks, about 29,000 player-matches) from the vaastav FPL archive, with market-average closing odds from football-data.co.uk; 2026/27 GW2–5 held out, with Kalshi prices as they stood 60 minutes before each deadline.
  • Every gameweek is rebuilt from information available before it: player totals, results and lineup history up to the previous gameweek only. Checks: FPL’s points reproduced for every 2025/26 player-match, every score reconciled, no future information.
  • No injury flags exist historically, so everyone counts as available. The live agent has flags, so it should do better on availability than shown here.
  • 2025/26 closing odds are set slightly after FPL’s deadline, so they know a little more than the live agent will.
  • FPL’s own archived expected points were not used as a benchmark: missing for 27 of 38 gameweeks, and correlating 0.74–0.83 with actual points where present, which indicates they were recorded after the matches.
